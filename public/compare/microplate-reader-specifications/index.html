7 Practical fluorescence dynamic range in one gain setting, without re-reading the plate

Take a plate whose brightest and dimmest wells are a million-fold apart. Readers that pick a gain per well inside the read see both at once; readers that fix a gain per read see one end and clip or lose the other, and the fix is a second pass. The Spark and Neo2 can widen effective range by reading the plate twice; that doubles the read time and breaks kinetic timing.

Practical fluorescence dynamic range in one gain setting, without re-reading the plate
ReaderSpecification or findingNotes and conditions
CLARIOstar Plus

8 decades in a single measurement (Enhanced Dynamic Range): a test flash picks a factory-calibrated gain per well, inside the delay the read already has.

Works in kinetics, with injectors and with a stacker. The user never sets a gain. Flying mode, the fastest read, runs at a fixed gain without EDR; in standard mode EDR’s test flash fits inside the pause the read already takes.

EnVision Nexus

No gain to set on the Ultra-sensitive photon counter and no published count-rate ceiling. Filter-path PMTs: no gain setting in any Kaleido 4.1 operation; the only bright-sample control it names is an “Attenuated” filter-module property.

None of the ten operation parameter lists in the Kaleido 4.1 manual (Rev E) has a gain, PMT-voltage or sensitivity entry, and the 2026 filter list (2321302) shows no module marked attenuated.

PHERAstar FSX

8 decades in one visit to the well (EDR). 700,000,000 counts.

Works in kinetics and with a stacker. Flying mode, the fastest read, runs at a fixed gain without EDR; in standard mode EDR’s test flash fits inside the pause the read already takes.

SpectraMax iD3 / iD5

Kinetics run at a fixed gain chosen before the run, “a nominal dynamic range of three decades” per gain (MD, 2026). Endpoints get AutoPMT: a pre-read at High, then re-reads of saturated wells at Medium and Low.

MD’s own Figure 3: High saturates at 33 nM, Medium at 333 nM, Low is “losing sensitivity at low concentration.” The iD5 widens range for endpoints by re-reading saturated wells and, in fluorescence, by adjusting lamp intensity; it can’t do that in a kinetic or in luminescence. “For kinetic read, the PMT settings have to be fixed”; each gain covers “a nominal dynamic range of three decades.”

Synergy H1

A single read tops out at 99,999 RFU; the manual’s remedy for saturation is “Try lowering the gain/sensitivity” (read again). BioTek’s 2010 table gave 5 decades; the 2026 sheet says 7 for the same model.

Gain is set per read, by number or scaled to a well the user picks as brightest.

Synergy Neo2

Fixed gain per read, ceiling 10 million counts. “Auto-ranging” runs a gain check on every well and adds minutes per plate. BioTek support: use manual gain “for kinetic tests, rapid reads or for tests that require high sensitivity.”

Extended range by re-reading is possible for endpoints; for flash luminescence BioTek’s own tips say it is “simply not possible.”

Tecan Spark

One gain, chosen before the read, about 3–4 decades usable (inferred from Tecan’s help text). Tecan’s “extended dynamic range” is “Two consecutive measurement runs with a high and a low amplification” with the listed drawback “Long measurement times.”

Every one of Tecan’s four gain modes has a printed “Disadvantages” panel. Optimal mode: “If signal intensities within one plate are very far spread, certain signals might not be detected any more.” Two passes means the same well is read twice, at two moments; a kinetic can’t hold its timing. The two-pass method does widen the effective range for endpoints. Extended range is “Two consecutive measurement runs”; its listed drawback: “Long measurement times.”

VANTAstar

8 decades in a single measurement (EDR), gain set per well in <0.1 s.

—
Varioskan LUX

Auto gain picks one of four calibrated gains per well from a trial flash. Product page commits to FI >6 decades; the tech note shows seven.

Whether auto gain runs in kinetic reads: not published. The gain is chosen before the signal moves.

9 Detectors: how many, what kind, and whether two colors can be read at the same instant

Simultaneous dual emission means the two halves of a ratio come from the same flash, so lamp flicker and timing drop out of the ratio. Only two readers here do it as standard, and only one does it on identical detectors and from the bottom.

CLARIOstar Plus

One low-noise PMT; red-shifted PMT option to 900 nm; CCD spectrometer for absorbance. Two colors read one after the other.

EnVision Nexus

Two PMTs on the filter path (230–850 nm), simultaneous dual channel via dual-channel module. One PMT, 400–650 nm, on the Ultra-sensitive/HT-Alpha module, no filters. Photodiode for absorbance.

PHERAstar FSX

Two matched pairs of PMTs (low-noise pair for FI/FP/lum; red-shifted pair for TR-FRET) plus a CCD spectrometer. Both channels of any ratio read at the same instant, top or bottom.

Simultaneous dual emission in FI, FP, luminescence, TR-FRET and Alpha, from the same flash, on identical detectors.

SpectraMax iD3 / iD5

One “ultra-cooled” PMT for every mode, emission to 850 nm. Two colors one after the other. Photocathode type not named.

A tube that reaches 850 nm is red-extended and carries high dark current; the cooling is what makes it usable. Field experience: in luminescence it is “just good enough to be bad.” No warm-up, stability or drift figure is published.

Synergy H1

One PMT per light path (one monochromator, one filter cube); photodiode for absorbance; red-shifted option to 900 nm. Two colors one after the other.

Synergy Neo2

Up to three PMTs. The two top detectors are different types: one extended into the red, one lower-noise that “isn’t responsive above 700nm.” Bottom: one PMT. Simultaneous dual emission top only, Dual Filter Top builds only, and the cube for it is special order (“please contact BioTek”).

BioTek’s own installation test allows the red-shifted tube ten times the luminescence detection limit of the low-noise one (≤500 vs ≤50 amol). Choose two blue tubes and TR-FRET suffers; two red and BRET is ruined; one of each and neither is good.

Tecan Spark

One fluorescence PMT, one photon-counting luminescence PMT, a photodiode for absorbance. Every two-color fluorescence ratio is two reads. NanoBRET: donor then acceptor, 300 ms each.

—
VANTAstar

One low-noise PMT; CCD spectrometer. Two colors read one after the other. No red-shifted option.

—
Varioskan LUX

One PMT for every mode. Two colors one after the other. Luminescence calibration includes a dark-level drift check and expires every 10 minutes.

11 Monochromator design: how it selects color and how it blocks the wrong color

Stray light is lamp light that reaches the detector; it sets the background every dim sample has to beat. A grating leaks about one part in a thousand (Tecan’s own figure), which is why grating readers add a second grating or steer sensitive assays to filters.

Monochromator design: how it selects color and how it blocks the wrong color
ReaderSpecification or findingNotes and conditions
CLARIOstar Plus

Two linear variable filter (LVF) slides per monochromator, no grating, no slit. Light outside the band must pass two overlapping filters.

Tecan’s 2023 sales slide said LVFs give “less defined peaks”; BMG’s answer is to show the spectral scans.

EnVision Nexus

Not applicable: filter modules only. The manual: “It is advisable to insert the filter module in position 1 in order to obtain the best results.”

The slots are not equal.

PHERAstar FSX

Not applicable. Filters on a free-air light path, no light guides.

SpectraMax iD3 / iD5

Grating monochromator. Blocking: the only stray-light figure published is for absorbance, ≤0.05% at 260 nm and 280 nm; no out-of-band rejection figure is given for the fluorescence or luminescence monochromators. MD, 2004 (GEMINI line): “a considerable amount of lamp light is reflected into emission beam. Raw background is much higher than in cuvette.”

The iD3 guide (Table A-2, p. 106) gives the same absorbance figure; the newer iD5e guide and brochure list it at 230 nm and 280 nm. The 2004 deck predates the iD series; the geometry it describes is the same.

Synergy H1

Grating monochromators. Blocking figure: not published. The error table, on a monochromator read: “the luminescence probe may be picking up stray light. Try installing a plug in the filter cube.”

Stray light can reach the detector, and the fix is a part in the cube.

Synergy Neo2

Grating monochromator on its own light path; Agilent calls it a “quad monochromator.” Out-of-band blocking figure for the fluorescence path: not published. The only stray-light number Agilent prints is for absorbance: 0.03% at 230 nm.

A BioTek-era Neo2 brochure (undated, hosted by a Harvard core facility) says “Four holographic gratings are used to eliminate stray light” but its own spec table lists “Double grating monochromators (top/bottom)”; neither version gives an OD or percent rejection figure.

Tecan Spark

Grating monochromators (“QuadX”); Tecan’s guide says the fluorescence module uses “double monochromators.” Blocking: 10⁶ for a double monochromator (10³ for a single one), a generic figure with no Spark-specific measurement. Tecan on the single-monochromator 10³: “this blocking is often not sufficient” for fluorescence.

This is the Spark’s own guide (v2.2, p. 163), the same passage the matrix had quoted from the Infinite 200 manual; superscripts are flattened to “103” and “106” in the text layer, and the guide’s worked example (10.000 counts vs 10 counts) confirms 10³.

VANTAstar

LVF, no grating, no slit. BMG: LVFs “eliminate the stray light issues found in conventional monochromators.”

—
Varioskan LUX

Gratings and slits; light guides carry light to and from the well. Light is lost at four points (excitation slit, delivery optic, emission light guide, emission slit).

Thermo’s 17 nm minimum separation rule exists “to prevent any stray light from the excitation light from interfering with the emission light.”

12 Are fixed dichroic mirrors needed on the monochromator path?

A dichroic is the mirror that sends excitation down to the plate and lets emission back up; it works for one color band. Tecan says in print that the dichroic is where the sensitivity comes from, then ships one color of it. The readers with no dichroic on the monochromator path use a 50% mirror or none, and lose light. Only the LVD tracks the dye.
